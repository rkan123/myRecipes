# Butter Chicken

## notes

There are a few Indian-specific ingredients here that you might not have in your pantry. It's okay to skip them or substitute them as long as you understand what they add to a curry. For example, traditionally butter chicken exclusively uses chillies from a region in India called Kashmir. These chillies are not spicy, and they have a deep red color which gives the curry its characteristic orange color. You can use smoked paprika instead. It gives your curry a smoky touch which is different but nice. You can also use chipotle peppers, which give your curry a super earthy flavor. But I hope you know how hot your chillies are and how much you are comfortable with. Use this recipe as a template to make a dish that satisfies your palate :D

You can also absolutely experiment with your spices. I like adding cloves, cardamoms and cinnamon sticks. You can explore those and also star anise, red/black pepper, fennel seeds, or whatever comes to mind. YOU might make the next Michelin-star butter chicken; who knows, right!?

Season your dish as you go. You added onions? Add some salt! You put in tomatoes? Add some salt! It is very beneficial to add salt in stages because:

1. You season each stage and the salt has time to spread through the dish evenly. You will also know how salty or bland your dish is at each stage, so you can add ingredients accordingly. If you put too much salt in your onions stage, then maybe you can add more tomatoes, or maybe add more meat to balance that out later.
2. Salt also helps you cook. Salt helps draw out moisture, which can be very beneficial. For example, while frying your onions, salt draws out their moisture, which helps you cook and caramelize them much faster.

For marination, overnight is best. But if you are doing it overnight, use only yoghurt as your source of acid, and add lime juice only a couple of minutes before you cook your meat.
The acid in limes + yoghurt tenderizes your meat too much if left overnight, and your meat will be mushy when cooked.
4 hours with yoghurt + lemon juice is 90% as good as overnight with yoghurt.
If you are making this last minute, try to at least give the meat 30 minutes with yoghurt + lime juice. If you know what you are doing, you can also add papaya extract here to make marination quick af.

## Ingredients

### the meat

- 1 kg chicken thigh, skinless and boneless
- 2 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
- 2 tsp salt
- 1/2 lime (squeezed)
- 1 tbsp oil (any oil is fine but neutral-flavored is preferred)
- 2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder (or any chilli powder that you have. A variant that is not very hot but has a nice red color is good)
- 2 tsp coriander powder
- 2 tsp cumin powder
- 1 tsp turmeric powder
- 1 palmful of kasoori methi (this is optional. You can skip this in the marinade)
- 1/2 tsp amchur powder (this is super optional)
- 1/2 tsp black salt (substitute this with 1 tsp of MSG if you have it on hand, otherwise skip)
- 200 g yoghurt (any acidic plain yoghurt variant is fine)

### the sauce

- 2 tbsp ghee or clarified butter (it's okay if you don't have it)
- 2 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 large onions (sliced or diced, doesn't matter) (in total you should have about 300-400 grams)
- 2 tbsp ginger-garlic paste (if you don't have this, add about 6-8 cloves of crushed garlic and about an inch of peeled ginger root, also crushed)
- 1-5 green chillies (depending on how spicy you want it)
- 2-3 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder (or the same substitute you used for the marinade)
- 2 tsp coriander powder
- 1/2-1 tsp turmeric powder
- 3-5 dry red chillies (translates to around 1-2 tbsp of crushed chilli flakes)
- 12 green cardamom pods
- 3-4 cloves
- 1/2 inch cinnamon stick
- 1000 g tomatoes (I really love using canned San Marzano tomatoes)
- a handful of roasted cashew nuts (idk, like 15-20 cashews maybe)
- 1 bunch of coriander leaves, roughly chopped (if you have them. Otherwise, parsley with lemon zest and juice added to substitute)
- Salt to taste
- 100 g double cream (or as much as you want ;) )
- 80 g salted butter
- a handful of kasoori methi (this is dried fenugreek leaves. It's an amazing ingredient but it kinda smells like weed lol)
- 5 g garam masala (whatever variant of garam masala you can find. If you can't find any, you can make some, or skip this entirely)
- Honey to taste (about 1 tsp to start) or sugar

## Method

1. **Marinate meat:** In a large bowl, mix the oil, salt, chilli powder, coriander, cumin, turmeric, kasoori methi, amchur, black salt, ginger-garlic paste, lime and yoghurt into a smooth paste. Add the chicken and coat it thoroughly. Cover with film and marinate in the fridge for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight.
2. **Grill the chicken:** Skewer the chicken and grill/bake until the surface is nicely charred, like brown to black on the surface but not fully burnt. In an oven, use a rack at 250°C (480°F) or a broiler or your highest setting.
	1. You can choose to NOT skewer the meat as well and just leave the chicken on a baking tray/pan. But make sure not to overcrowd your pan, as the yoghurt and meat will release a bunch of water. You want your meat to char, not boil.
	2. If you have a torch, just put your meat on a tray and blast the fuck out of it. You don't need to cook the chicken here; just char the surface.
	3. If this is too much effort, just add the marinated meat to the pot later.
3. **Temper your spices and red chillies:** For the sauce, add your ghee/oil to a pot with the heat turned on low. Add the cumin seeds, cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and red chillies, and fry until they crackle. Make sure your heat is low as you don't want your spices to burn. You can also add the spices while the oil is heating up.
4. **Add aromatics:** Add your onions and green chillies, and turn your heat to medium. Fry until the onions are slightly brown. Add like a teaspoon of salt here. Then add the garlic and ginger. Cook this until you can no longer smell the ginger and garlic in the pot (around 3-5 mins).
5. **Add dry spices to pot:** Now add your dry/ground spices. Add the chilli powder, coriander powder and turmeric powder and fry them with the onions for 2-3 mins.
	1. After this stage, if you are using a steel pot, you might have a lot of fond built up at the bottom of the pot. You can deglaze this with some wine to add some flavor and reduce until all the alcohol evaporates. I am no expert in gastronomy but I find that:
		1. Red wine adds some fruitiness and depth which pairs well with tomatoes in the curry.
		2. White wine makes the curry seem cleaner and lighter. (I also use the cheapest wine off the grocery shelves for cooking.)
6. **Add tomatoes and cashews:** Now add tomatoes, cashews, some sugar/honey and a pinch of salt. Cook on low heat for 10-45 minutes, until the tomatoes are completely soft. The cook time depends on what kind of tomatoes you are cooking with. For canned San Marzano, 20 mins is enough. If at this stage your pot is getting dry and you're burning the sauce at the bottom, just add a little bit of water.
7. **Blend the sauce:** Take a few minutes here to fish out any of the whole spices that you can find (cardamoms, cinnamon, cloves). These are what make your sauce gritty because they don't blend well. Blend everything until completely smooth. Strain through a fine sieve if you want it extra silky (super optional, high-effort step).
8. _If you have not grilled your chicken:_ Leave the sauce in your blender or in a different container and cook the chicken in your sauce pot until it's 90% cooked. Add the sauce back at this stage and finish with the ingredients mentioned in the next step.
9. **Finish:** Return the sauce to the pot and add some butter. Add your cream. Taste the curry and add salt if you have to. Finally, add the grilled chicken and simmer gently until you are convinced that the chicken is fully cooked. Adjust the salt to taste and add garam masala and kasoori methi (take some of it in your palm and rub it against your other palm to crush it before adding). Don't boil hard once the cream is in, or it may split. Finish with some chopped coriander leaves at the end ^^

And you are done. At this step, you can pat yourself on the back for a job well done. You can serve this with some naan or some nice basmati rice. I find that rice spiced with cumin, bay leaves and star anise goes amazingly with butter chicken. But then again, the world is your oyster. Be imaginative. Maybe butter chicken pizza? Pasta? Lasagna? Burrito?

If you followed this recipe, your curry should be absolutely divine. But if it's not, that's okay too. You still put a lot of effort into a dish, and I hope that the people who eat your dish, and you yourself, can enjoy and respect your efforts.
